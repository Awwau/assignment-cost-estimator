Swiss Income Tax Methodology and Sources

Hypothetical Swiss income tax is estimated for a single employee living in Zurich, Switzerland, using the employee's annual salary and bonus.

The estimate includes:

  • Direct federal tax: calculated using progressive Swiss federal income tax brackets.
  • Zurich cantonal tax: calculated using the assumed Zurich cantonal tax schedule and multiplier of 95%.
  • Zurich municipal tax: calculated using the assumed City of Zurich municipal multiplier of 119%.
  • Personal tax: an assumed annual charge of CHF 24.

Swiss employee AHV/IV/EO and unemployment insurance contributions are calculated separately and deducted when estimating taxable income.

Important assumption: The model uses simplified employment expense deductions. It does not represent a complete Swiss tax return. Additional deductions, individual circumstances and official rounding rules may affect the final tax amount.

Employment expense deduction: The model applies CHF 4,000 annually as a general employment expense allowance. The published 2026 Zurich tax parameters specify 3% of qualifying net salary, subject to a CHF 2,000 minimum and CHF 4,000 maximum. Official 2026 source: Canton of Zurich - Employment Expense Rules 2026

This confirms the general expense allowance, not the completeness of the employee's taxable income calculation. Other deductions remain outside the simplified estimate.

Official Swiss tax sources:

  • Swiss Federal Tax Administration (FTA) - federal income tax guidance and tax calculators.
  • Federal Law on Direct Federal Tax - direct federal income tax law.
  • Tax Calculation and Tax Rates - City of Zurich - cantonal and municipal tax information and tax rates.

UK Income Tax Rules and Sources

The estimate uses the England income tax rules for the 2026/27 tax year, applied to both assignment years as a modelling assumption.

  • Personal allowance: £12,570, reduced by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income above £100,000.
  • Basic rate: 20% on the first £37,700 of taxable income.
  • Higher rate: 40% on taxable income above the basic-rate band, up to the additional-rate threshold.
  • Additional rate: 45% on taxable income above £125,140.
  • Employer-paid UK income tax is grossed up iteratively because the employer's tax payment is treated as additional taxable income under the model.

Official sources: GOV.UK- Income Tax rates and allowances | GOV.UK - Rates and thresholds for employers 2026/27

The second assignment year uses the same tax parameters for comparison. Actual 2027/28 rules should be checked when available. The gross-up follows the methodology specified in the assignment brief.

Tax Equalisation Methodology: The calculation follows the four-step approach described by Andrew Bailey: estimate hypothetical home-country tax, deduct it from standard pay, add assignment allowances, and calculate host-country tax on the resulting assignment pay. The model also accounts for employer-paid UK tax through an iterative gross-up calculation.

Methodology reference: Andrew Bailey - What Is Tax Equalisation?, International HR Adviser, pp. 25–26

Work Visa and Sponsorship

Work authorisation: A Swiss national requires appropriate UK VISA to undertake a two-year employment assignment in the United Kingdom.

Potential visa route: Global Business Mobility - Senior or Specialist Worker. This route allows eligible employees to transfer to a linked UK business.

Eligibility Requirements

  • The UK business must hold the appropriate Home Office sponsor licence.
  • The employee must receive a valid Certificate of Sponsorship.
  • The proposed UK role must have an eligible occupation code.
  • Salary must meet the applicable threshold: at least £52,500 annually or the occupation's going rate, whichever is higher.
  • If the employee is earning less than £73,900 a year, they must have worked for their employer outside the UK for at least 12 months.
  • The employee must meet the overseas employment requirements.

Assessment for this test case

The employee has three years of prior employment and annual salary and bonus of CHF 170,000 (~GBP 154545.45). These facts support potential eligibility. However, the employee's occupation code, qualifying sponsored salary, and the UK subsidiary's sponsorship status must still be verified before eligibility can be confirmed.

Official guidance: GOV.UK - Senior or Specialist Worker

Business Visit Rules

Business visit status: Swiss nationals can generally visit the UK for permitted short-term business activities without obtaining a Standard Visitor visa, using an ETA.

Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA)

Citizens from Switzerland require an electronic travel authorisation (ETA) for short visits (up to 6 months) to the UK unless exempt. However, an employee who already holds a valid UK work visa does not need a separate ETA. An ETA is not a substitute for a work visa.

Permitted Business Activities

  • Attending business meetings and conferences.
  • Negotiating and signing contracts.
  • Attending interviews and seminars.
  • Conducting permitted site visits and inspections.

Restrictions

  • Visitors generally cannot undertake ordinary paid or unpaid employment for a UK company.
  • Visitors cannot fill a position within a UK organisation, unless a specific permitted activity exception applies.
  • Standard Visitors can normally stay for up to six months per visit.
  • Visitors cannot use frequent or successive visits to live in the UK.

Official sources:

  • GOV.UK - Visit on business
  • GOV.UK - ETA permitted activities
Passport and Entry Requirements

Passport: The Swiss employee should hold a valid passport for travel to the UK. The passport should remain valid throughout the intended stay.

Before Travelling

  • Obtain approval for the appropriate UK work visa before starting the assignment.
  • Complete the identity verification process required for the visa application.
  • Check the UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) account and confirm that the eVisa is valid and details are accurate. If you do not already have a UKVI account, you need to set up access to your eVisa.
  • Ensure the current passport is linked to the UKVI account.

Entry into the UK

The employee should travel with their valid passport and ensure that their immigration permission is available through their UKVI account.

Swiss nationals with eligible biometric passports and valid immigration status can use automated ePassport gates at the UK border on arrival.

Official Sources

Employer Obligations

The UK subsidiary must meet Home Office sponsorship requirements before and during the employee's assignment.

Before the Assignment

  • Hold a valid sponsor licence covering the relevant Global Business Mobility route.
  • Confirm that the Swiss company and UK subsidiary meet the qualifying business relationship requirements.
  • Check the employee's occupation code, eligible role, salary and visa requirements.
  • Assign a valid Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS) through the sponsorship system.
  • Pay the applicable sponsorship fees and any Immigration Skills Charge.
  • Complete the required right-to-work check before the employee starts working.

During the Assignment

  • Maintain accurate employee records, including contact details and evidence of immigration permission.
  • Monitor the employee's attendance and compliance with their sponsored role.
  • Report relevant changes, such as unauthorised absences, changes to employment, or early termination, within the applicable deadlines.
  • Keep the required sponsorship documents and cooperate with Home Office compliance checks.
